# Feature Format Matching

This reference operates **after a relevant SERP feature has been identified**. Its job is to translate the feature's current presentation into an eligible on-page structure without pretending that formatting alone guarantees inclusion. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Feature-specific procedure
For featured snippets, determine whether the current result is a paragraph, list, table, or another extractable form, then make the answer clear and directly aligned with the query. For People Also Ask, use genuine related questions and answer them accurately within the page's natural structure. For rich-result features, first verify eligibility and structured-data requirements. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

Do not optimize around an arbitrary word-count target. The correct length is the amount of information needed to answer accurately and safely in the observed format. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Concrete scenario
A query produces a list-style featured snippet for a procedural task. The page rewrites its relevant section as a clearly ordered procedure with descriptive step labels, prerequisites, and verification. The team does not claim that the structure guarantees the snippet. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Failure modes
Adding fake questions, using structured data on content that does not qualify, hiding required information from users, and treating one observed SERP layout as permanent. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Evidence boundary
Feature eligibility and format guidance come from published Search documentation where available. Prediction of inclusion is uncertain; claims of guaranteed placement are not permitted. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Feature eligibility gate
Before changing markup or page structure, confirm what feature is actually present, whether the page is eligible, and what visible content would support it. For structured-data features, compare the markup with the content users can see and follow Google's eligibility requirements. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

Treat observed inclusion as a result to measure, not a guarantee. If a snippet disappears after an edit, compare the query, answer structure, page version, and competitor result before attributing the change to one formatting choice. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]
