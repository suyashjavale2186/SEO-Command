# Competitor Selection

This reference defines **which competitors belong in a research set** before the analyst compares their pages. The goal is comparability, not prestige: a useful benchmark shares enough of the target's search task and market context to support a meaningful comparison. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Selection test
Classify candidates by page role, query intent, audience, geography, business model, and evidence posture. Keep content competitors separate from direct commercial competitors and from institutional sources. Record the reason each candidate was chosen so the set cannot be changed silently to support a preferred conclusion. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete example
For “employee payroll software India,” a regional payroll vendor and an independent review site may be strong content comparators, while a government tax page is better treated as a factual source. A U.S.-only vendor may be commercially relevant but still be a poor page-level benchmark for Indian search intent. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Use of selected competitors
Once the set is clean, compare format, evidence, information gaps, internal linking, product facts, and authority context. Do not assume that every difference explains ranking. A difference becomes a hypothesis to investigate, not an established cause. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
Avoid selecting only famous domains, mixing countries, treating all results as interchangeable, or choosing competitors after seeing the result you want to explain. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Competitor selection supports observation; it cannot expose a private Google weighting system. Any causal explanation beyond the evidence is **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
## Practical handoff for competitor selection
The analyst should leave a compact handoff that identifies the exact artifact, the evidence owner, the user or business outcome at stake, and the next validation action. For **competitor selection**, record the observable state before interpretation so that a second practitioner can reproduce the decision without relying on the author's memory. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Use a topic-specific stop condition: pause when the evidence source is stale, the artifact is not comparable to the benchmark, or the proposed action depends on an undocumented Google mechanism. When an action is approved, preserve the rationale and the uncertainty so a later measurement cycle can test whether the decision was useful. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
