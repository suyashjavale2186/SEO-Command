## Operating context

This reference supports the `keyword-opportunity` skill when it handles **demand evidence**. Use the named artifact or decision as the unit of analysis and preserve provenance before acting. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Analyst worksheet
Record demand evidence from the tools actually available: Search Console, keyword databases, customer language, historical query data, and current SERPs. Do not manufacture precise volume where the source does not provide it. Separate observed demand from seasonal or market-driven movement and state the limitations of the dataset before turning it into an opportunity estimate. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Realistic scenario
A developer-tooling company sees a rise in internal support searches for “API rate limit examples,” while a commercial keyword platform reports modest demand. The opportunity note preserves both signals: first-party demand suggests a meaningful audience need, while external volume remains uncertain. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Reasons to reject the inference
Treating one volume estimate as ground truth; comparing incompatible keyword databases; ignoring regional or seasonal effects; or turning “high volume” into “high business value” without intent evidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Watch for the characteristic mistake of **demand evidence**: applying the concept to the wrong artifact, treating a proxy metric as the outcome, copying a competitor pattern without proving comparability, or turning a hypothesis into an assertion. Also stop when the proposed action would require fabricated evidence, violate search-policy guidance, or imply a guaranteed ranking or revenue result. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
