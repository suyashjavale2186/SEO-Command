### Worked scenarios

**Comparison:** For “best payroll software for 50-person companies,” open with the decision criteria, compare meaningful alternatives on those criteria, explain trade-offs, and state which business conditions change the recommendation. Support each product fact with current evidence or clearly label supplied business information. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**How-to:** For “how to migrate from CRM A to CRM B,” put prerequisites and sequence near the task start, distinguish irreversible steps from reversible ones, and include validation points that let the operator verify each stage before proceeding. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**FAQ:** Use the questions that genuinely arise from the page's subject or audience evidence. Give direct answers, then add qualification or context where a short answer could mislead. Do not manufacture questions merely to expand semantic coverage. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


### Execution checklist

- Re-read the selected content format and state the reader's job before drafting. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
- Use the brief's evidence map as the source of claims; do not substitute invented experience. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
- Make transitions serve the chosen format: comparisons move across criteria, procedures move across steps, guides move across decisions. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
- Keep tables, examples, and callouts only when they materially improve comprehension. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

### Failure patterns


Every material recommendation about format or execution must use the required recommendation block. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Decision procedure
Translate the search task into an explicit content job, then select the format, evidence requirements, and business role. Use the SERP to understand expectations, but reserve space for first-party material that competitors are unlikely to reproduce. The brief should tell the writer what must be true and what evidence is still missing. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Case study
A comparison brief for “best payroll software for a 50-person company” contains buyer criteria, current product facts, a decision matrix, and an internal operator interview. It does not ask the writer to reproduce every competitor heading. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Common failure modes
Briefing from keywords alone; specifying an article length instead of a user task; making “cover everything” the only content requirement; or approving a topic before its evidence and conversion role are clear. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
