# Quality Scoring for Publication

Use this reference to judge a draft **before publication**. The editorial task is to decide whether the finished page is useful, evidence-backed, original in a meaningful sense, and faithful to the user's task. This is not a proxy for a private Google quality score. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Review sequence
Start by writing the user's job in plain language. Check whether the opening and page structure help complete that job directly. Next sample the claims most likely to change a decision: current specifications, pricing, regulations, technical behavior, comparative judgments, and outcome statements. Trace each to a source or mark it for verification. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

Then look for information advantage. Record the actual differentiators: first-party observations, expert explanations, original data, calculations, methodology, or transparent limitations. “More detail” alone is not evidence of originality. Finally inspect format fidelity: a comparison should compare, a procedure should be executable, and a buying guide should explain the decision criteria before recommending options. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete example
A draft at `https://example.com/guides/warehouse-software` says Vendor A is “the fastest” and claims the author “tested every platform.” No test record exists. The editor removes the first-person claim, requests current vendor evidence for performance statements, and rewrites the recommendation around observable criteria such as workflow fit and documented integrations. The page becomes more defensible even though it says less. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Publication blockers
Stop publication when a central claim cannot be verified, the author is represented as having experience they do not have, the page's main promise is unsupported, or the planned structure exists mainly to repeat search phrases. Do not invent testimonials, screenshots, credentials, testing, or customer results. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Editorial failure modes
Do not treat page length as quality, equate polished prose with expertise, or reward a competitor-shaped outline without asking what the reader actually needs. Do not use AI-detector scores as the definition of quality. Optimize for evidence of real thinking, usefulness, expertise, originality, and honest limitations. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Unverified claims about Google's private ranking mechanisms are **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. Every recommendation from the parent skill must use the centralized recommendation contract. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
