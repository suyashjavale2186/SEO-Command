# Quality Scoring

Use this reference to judge a **draft that is about to be published**. The job is editorial: determine whether the finished page is useful, accurate, original, complete enough for its search task, and honest about its evidence. It is not a proxy for Google's private scoring systems. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## What the editor must inspect
Read the page as a user first. Can the reader identify the answer, procedure, comparison, or decision aid without fighting the structure? Then inspect factual claims, first-party evidence, attribution, examples, and unsupported assertions. Quality scoring should expose weaknesses that an author can fix before publication. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Editorial scoring sequence
**1. Task completion.** State the query or user job in one sentence and mark every material step needed to complete it. Do not reward extra sections that do not help the job. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**2. Evidence integrity.** Sample the important claims. For product specifications, pricing, legal requirements, technical behavior, or current facts, locate the source or mark the claim for verification. Never accept invented testing, testimonials, screenshots, credentials, or first-person stories. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

**3. Information advantage.** Highlight what the page contributes that a competent competitor would not automatically reproduce: first-party data, expert experience, original examples, useful calculations, transparent methodology, or a clearly reasoned synthesis. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**4. Editorial usefulness.** Check headings, tables, examples, caveats, terminology, and transitions against the reader's likely decisions. A concise answer can be stronger than a longer one when it solves the task directly. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**5. Approval gate.** Classify the draft as publish, revise, or hold. “Hold” is appropriate when a critical claim cannot be verified or the page's main promise is materially unsupported. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete scenario
For `https://example.com/guides/vendor-comparison`, the draft claims that Vendor A is “the fastest” and says the team “tested every product.” The editor requests the test artifact and methodology. No artifact exists, so the first-person claim is removed and the comparison is rewritten around verifiable specifications plus an explicit limitation. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Failure modes
- Treating page length as a quality score.
- Rewarding keyword repetition when it makes prose less natural.
- Giving an “originality” score without identifying what is actually original.
- Passing unsupported claims because they appear plausible.
- Mistaking polished formatting for trustworthy evidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Do not infer a hidden Google quality score from this rubric. Any mechanism claim about Google that is not officially documented is **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. Every recommendation emitted by the parent skill must use the required recommendation block and evidence-status field. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
