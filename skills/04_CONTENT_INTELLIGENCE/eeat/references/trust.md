- # YMYL Scoring Rubric [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- Elevated E-E-A-T standards for Your Money or Your Life content. Use this rubric instead of the main 40-point rubric when the topic can affect a reader's health, finances, safety, legal standing, or civic participation. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- ## Google's YMYL definition [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines define YMYL as topics that "could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of people, or the welfare or well-being of society." The guidelines break this into three harm categories: [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- - **YMYL Health or Safety.** Mental, physical, or emotional health, or any form of safety. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- - **YMYL Financial Security.** Anything that could damage a person's ability to support themselves or their family. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Operating context

This reference supports the `eeat` skill when it handles **trust**. Use the named artifact or decision as the unit of analysis and preserve provenance before acting. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence map
Assess Experience, Expertise, Authority, and Trust as distinct evidence questions. Experience concerns genuine first-hand involvement; Expertise concerns subject competence; Authority concerns credible standing; Trust concerns transparent sourcing, identity, incentives, and limitations. Never fill a missing pillar with a fabricated biography or invented case. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Practical case
A financial guide uses an accountant as the named reviewer but never explains the reviewer’s role or sources the tax claims. The improvement is to disclose authorship and review scope, source regulated claims, and clearly separate practitioner commentary from general information. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Disqualifiers
Generic “expert” bios; unverifiable credentials; anonymous testimonials; invented first-person testing; or treating backlink metrics as proof that an author has subject expertise. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
