## Authority in editorial context

For E-E-A-T review, **Authority** concerns whether the creator and organization have credible standing for the subject, while keeping authority separate from the presence of backlinks or any single SEO metric. Examine authorship, professional role, relevant published work, organizational identity, transparent credentials, and corroborating references. The goal is to establish whether readers have sensible reasons to trust the source for the topic—not to manufacture an authority signal. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

### Worked scenario

A medical-device explainer is authored by a product marketer with no disclosed clinical qualification. The company can still publish useful product information, but the authority assessment should not blur marketing expertise with clinical expertise. A stronger version might add a qualified reviewer where appropriate, clearly separate manufacturer claims from independent clinical evidence, and disclose the author's actual role. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

### Evidence to inspect

- **Author identity:** Can the reader understand who created or reviewed the material and why that person is relevant? [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
- **Organizational legitimacy:** Is the business or publication clearly identified and transparent about its role? [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
- **Corroboration:** Are important claims supported by appropriate sources or first-party documentation? [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
- **Limitations:** Does the page disclose conflicts, sponsorship, product relationships, or areas outside the author's expertise? [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

### Red flags

Vague biographies, borrowed credentials, anonymous “expert” quotes, fabricated reviewers, and badges that cannot be verified should lower trust. Do not replace missing authority with keyword optimization or decorative awards. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

When authority evidence is weak, recommend a concrete verification step rather than declaring the page untrustworthy as a whole. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Implementation guide
Inspect referring pages in context: why the link exists, what the anchor says, where it is placed, whether the referring site is relevant, and how the relationship was acquired. Metrics such as DR or DA can be comparative vendor indicators, but they are not Google ranking scores. Risk is about the observed acquisition pattern, not the raw link count. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Specific example
A software site has hundreds of profile links but only a handful of editorial references from industry research. The audit separates the profile links from the citations and uses the latter as a stronger qualitative signal of topical relevance. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Red flags
Counting every referring domain equally; assuming a large link spike proves manipulation; treating one high-authority citation as causal proof; or recommending paid/manipulative links primarily to influence ranking signals. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
