- # YMYL Scoring Rubric [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- Elevated E-E-A-T standards for Your Money or Your Life content. Use this rubric instead of the main 40-point rubric when the topic can affect a reader's health, finances, safety, legal standing, or civic participation. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- ## Google's YMYL definition [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines define YMYL as topics that "could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of people, or the welfare or well-being of society." The guidelines break this into three harm categories: [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- - **YMYL Health or Safety.** Mental, physical, or emotional health, or any form of safety. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- - **YMYL Financial Security.** Anything that could damage a person's ability to support themselves or their family. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Operating context

This reference supports the `eeat` skill when it handles **expertise**. Use the named artifact or decision as the unit of analysis and preserve provenance before acting. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Control points
Ask for situations, not slogans. Strong prompts uncover what went wrong, what surprised the practitioner, which tradeoff mattered, who should not follow the advice, and what artifact or measurement supports the claim. Follow a broad answer with probes for time, setting, decision, result, and limitation. Obtain permission and confidentiality boundaries before publication. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Production example
Instead of “What is the best SEO process?”, ask “Tell me about a page update you reversed. What changed, what happened, and what made you abandon it?” The resulting account can supply a specific insight, while the factual parts can still be independently verified. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure signatures
Leading questions; converting opinion into research evidence; extracting confidential client details; or inventing first-person experience where the interview did not provide it. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Topic-specific operating notes
Treat **expertise** as the concrete object of analysis within **eeat**. Work from the artifact that the file names—rather than from a generic SEO checklist—and preserve the field-level evidence that another practitioner would need to reproduce the judgment. For this topic, inspect the relevant experience, expertise, authority, trust, verification, authorship, and transparent limitations signals first, then test the business or user consequence of the observed issue. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

When the evidence is incomplete, name the missing field or source explicitly and state what observation would change the conclusion. Do not substitute a vendor benchmark, an internal score, or a plausible Google explanation for missing evidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

The operator records the current state, isolates the specific variable relevant to **expertise**, compares it with the intended user/business outcome, and chooses an action that can later be validated. ### Topic-specific failure modes
Watch for the characteristic mistake of **expertise**: applying the concept to the wrong artifact, treating a proxy metric as the outcome, copying a competitor pattern without proving comparability, or turning a hypothesis into an assertion. Also stop when the proposed action would require fabricated evidence, violate search-policy guidance, or imply a guaranteed ranking or revenue result. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
