# Information Gain in Content Briefs

**Scope.** This reference is for `content-brief` when comparing the planned page with existing search results to find **reader-relevant information the draft can add**, not for conducting the expert interview or storing the site's entity graph. Its output is a briefing delta: what the page must contribute beyond obvious coverage. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Gap-finding method

Start by extracting the dominant questions answered across the strongest comparable results. Then build a second layer containing exceptions, mechanisms, trade-offs, decision criteria, real examples, first-party evidence, and limitations. Do not confuse “different wording” with “new information.” A synonym, extra adjective, or longer introduction is not an information advantage. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Use an evidence ledger. For every proposed addition, record **what is missing**, **why a reader needs it**, **what source can support it**, and **where it will appear in the outline**. An item can be high-value because it resolves an important decision, not because it is unusual. A competitor may omit a warranty exclusion that materially changes a purchase decision; that is a stronger gap than an obscure trivia point. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Briefing test

The brief should be able to state its unique contribution in plain language: “This page will answer X, but also show Y using Z evidence.” If the sentence could describe every competitor page in the SERP, the brief has not yet found a meaningful information edge. Where no defensible edge exists, the strategist should consider a different topic or a better first-party research input rather than padding the outline. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]

## Concrete scenario

For “commercial heat-pump installation,” competitor pages explain equipment types and headline efficiency claims. A contractor can add decision-grade material from its own service records: site conditions that make a certain configuration unsuitable, the inspection questions asked before quoting, and maintenance constraints that affect total ownership cost. The brief assigns each insight to a section and identifies the evidence source. It does not claim that these additions guarantee higher rankings; they make the content more useful and distinct. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes

A semantic word list is not a gap analysis. Neither is copying competitor headings. Reject gaps that cannot be supported, are irrelevant to the reader's task, or depend on invented customer experiences. Also avoid describing an information-gap model as a public Google ranking score unless the source explicitly supports that claim. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]
