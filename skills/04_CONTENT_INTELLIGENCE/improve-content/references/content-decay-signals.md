# Content Decay Signals for Editors

This reference translates observed decay into **editorial diagnosis**. It belongs to `improve-content`, so it emphasizes what the writer should inspect inside the page once measurement shows a performance change. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Editorial symptoms
Look for outdated examples, expired offers, broken screenshots, changed product interfaces, missing newly common questions, obsolete terminology, unsupported claims, and sections that no longer match the current SERP task. Also check whether competitors have introduced information the original page never had. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Diagnosis sequence
First compare the page with its historical intent and current query set. Then compare important claims against current first-party sources. Finally compare the page's actual task coverage with the current result types. This sequence prevents an editor from refreshing wording while leaving the substantive problem untouched. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete scenario
A “best project management apps” article contains prices and integrations from an earlier product cycle. Search Console shows stable category impressions but weaker click performance. The editor verifies current product facts, refreshes the decision criteria, and removes discontinued products rather than rewriting every paragraph for freshness. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Failure modes
Refreshing dates without changing stale evidence, adding competitors without understanding the selection criteria, deleting useful historical context, and treating temporary ranking movement as proof that the edit worked. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
A decay signal is an observed change, not proof of a specific Google mechanism. Any undocumented mechanism explanation is **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Editor handoff
The measurement signal should arrive with the exact query/page change, the likely content section affected, the evidence that became stale, and the business role of the page. That lets the editor make a targeted revision instead of treating “traffic down” as permission for a wholesale rewrite. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

Before changing copy, compare the current SERP against the period when the page was performing well. A shift from explanatory results to product-led results may indicate a changed task; stale dates alone would not explain that change. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
