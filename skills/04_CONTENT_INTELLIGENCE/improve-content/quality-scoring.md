# Quality Scoring for Existing Pages

This reference evaluates an **already-published page before and after an update**. The objective is to decide whether an edit preserves what already works while repairing a demonstrated weakness; it is not to grade pages against a universal writing score. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Baseline-first review
Start with the page's current query footprint, landing-page behavior, conversion role, links, and known source evidence. Separate successful elements from suspected defects. An update should not accidentally delete a useful section merely because it looks repetitive to an editor who lacks the performance context. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Update scoring sequence
**1. Preserve proven intent coverage.** Identify queries, SERP formats, and user tasks for which the page already earns visibility. Protect those portions unless evidence shows they are obsolete or misleading. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

**2. Diagnose the actual defect.** Classify the reason for the update: stale facts, weaker competitor coverage, missing first-party evidence, poor task completion, unclear structure, technical delivery issue, or changed business offer. Do not label every traffic decline “thin content.” [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**3. Score the proposed edit by expected trade-off.** Ask what will improve, what might regress, and what remains unchanged. Large rewrites deserve more caution because they can remove useful coverage that the analyst has not identified yet. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**4. Validate after publication.** Recheck the original signals alongside the intended improvement. Record whether the updated page better answers the user task, whether qualified traffic improved, and whether any important query or conversion behavior deteriorated. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Concrete scenario
A product-comparison page still receives strong impressions for category queries but has declining clicks after competitor pages add clearer pricing context. The update adds a verified pricing explanation and a better comparison table while preserving the page's existing category definitions. The editor does not replace the entire article merely because one section is outdated. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
- Rewriting a strong page from scratch without a baseline.
- Updating facts but leaving the underlying user task unresolved.
- Removing sections solely because they have low visible traffic.
- Treating a single post-update week as causal proof.
- Declaring success from rank movement while ignoring business outcomes. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Evidence boundary
This rubric evaluates editing decisions and observed outcomes; it does not reveal Google's hidden ranking formula. Unverified mechanism claims are **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**, not fact. Recommendations must use the centralized governance contract. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
