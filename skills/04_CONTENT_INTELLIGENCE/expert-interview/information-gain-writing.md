# Information Gain from Interviews

This reference handles **how interview material becomes publishable information advantage**. The interview is not valuable merely because it contains a first-person quote; it is valuable when the expert reveals decisions, constraints, edge cases, failures, or observations that materially improve the reader's understanding. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Extraction method
For each answer, tag the underlying information: a claim, example, process detail, trade-off, boundary condition, failure mode, measurement, or decision rationale. Then ask whether a competent competitor could reproduce the statement without access to the same experience or evidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Convert strong answers into one of four editorial forms: **explanation** (“why we chose X”), **procedure** (“what we actually did”), **case evidence** (“what happened when Y changed”), or **boundary** (“when this approach fails”). Preserve the expert's meaning and qualify opinions as opinions. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Follow-up prompts
When an answer is vague, ask: “What happened the last time?”, “What artifact proves that?”, “What did you change?”, “What was the trade-off?”, and “Who should not use this approach?” These prompts are designed to produce concrete material rather than polished marketing language. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete scenario
An engineer says, “Caching solved most of our build problem.” The writer asks which layer was cached, what symptom changed, what remained slow, and what evidence was recorded. The resulting section explains the actual bottleneck and limitation rather than publishing the vague sentence as proof. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
Turning opinion into fact, stripping away caveats, inventing a first-person anecdote when the interview did not supply one, or quoting the expert without explaining the decision context are common failures. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Evidence boundary
Interview material is first-party evidence only for what the interviewee actually establishes. It does not automatically prove external facts or causal mechanisms. Unverified Google-system explanations remain **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
