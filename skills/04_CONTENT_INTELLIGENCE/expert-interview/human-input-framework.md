# Human Input Framework for Interviews

This reference is the **interviewer's operating system**: how to elicit reliable first-party material and preserve it for later drafting. It is deliberately different from the content-brief version, which decides where human input belongs. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Interview flow
Open with the expert's role and the topic boundary. Move from broad recall to a recent concrete event, then to evidence. Ask what happened, what they decided, why, what they expected, what surprised them, what failed, and what they would now do differently. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Use “artifact probes” when answers become abstract: ask for a screenshot, configuration, report, calculation, example, log, or anonymized document when the expert is allowed to share it. Record confidentiality restrictions and publication permissions at capture time. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Core question bank
“What do most people get wrong?” “What happened in a real project?” “What surprised you?” “What failed?” “Who should NOT follow this advice?” Follow with topic-specific prompts for comparisons, implementations, reviews, and edge cases. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete scenario
During a payroll implementation interview, the expert initially says, “Integrations are the hard part.” The interviewer asks which integration failed, what dependency caused the problem, how it was diagnosed, and what evidence showed the fix worked. The final knowledge note contains a reproducible failure pattern rather than a generic warning. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
Leading the expert toward a predetermined conclusion, recording only polished success stories, forgetting permission boundaries, paraphrasing away important uncertainty, and presenting interview claims as independently verified facts are common mistakes. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Interview notes are first-party evidence of the interviewee's account, not automatic proof of external facts or search-engine behavior. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
