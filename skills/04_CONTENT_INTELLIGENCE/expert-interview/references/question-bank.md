# Expert interview question bank

This question bank is adapted from the original SuperSEO interview system. Its purpose is to extract real first-party knowledge that generic web research cannot supply. **[Tier 3 — carried-forward workflow]**

## Universal prompts
- What do most people get wrong about [topic]?
- Can you give me a specific example—a client, a project, a test, or a result?
- What surprised you when you actually did this?
- Who should NOT follow this advice, and why?

## Technical / how-to
- What error does almost everyone hit first?
- What step do beginners always skip?
- What did you waste the most time on when you first did this?
- What edge case breaks the usual tutorial?
- Which assumption in the official docs or common guidance is outdated in your environment?
- What is the fastest reliable way to tell something is wrong?
- What breaks at scale that does not break in a small example?
- What debugging move do you reach for when nothing works?

## Comparison / review
- Which would you recommend to a friend, and why?
- Where do reviewers usually get this comparison wrong?
- What does the marketing hide or understate?
- Which option is worse than it looks, and which is better than it looks?
- When would you recommend neither?
- What would have to happen for you to switch your recommendation?
- Which feature sounds important but does not matter in practice?
- What pricing or implementation gotcha should buyers know?
- What better comparison is nobody making?

## Thought leadership
- What conventional wisdom did you once believe but no longer do?
- What is the contrarian view you are willing to defend?
- What does your field currently misunderstand?
- Where do you think this is heading, and what evidence informs that view?
- What would change your mind?
- What are you uncertain about right now?
- What would you do differently if starting today?

## Case study / results
- Start with the actual outcome: what changed?
- What did you try first that did not work?
- When did you start to think it might fail?
- What was the unexpected lesson?
- What cost the most time or money?
- What was the turning point?
- What would you do differently next time?
- What evidence convinced you the intervention worked?

## Interview method
Ask one question at a time and adapt based on the answer. Prioritize specificity, context, trade-offs, failed attempts, surprises, and scope limitations. Do not fill unanswered questions with plausible text. **[Tier 4 internal method]**
