# Information Gain Writing from Expert Knowledge

**Scope.** This `expert-interview` reference governs the moment when interview material is transformed into **publishable first-party insight**. It is not a SERP-gap checklist; the interview system already has the raw evidence. The writing task is to preserve what the expert actually knows while turning it into useful explanations, examples, caveats, and decisions. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Source-to-sentence method

For every valuable interview answer, identify the smallest publishable proposition. Then locate its supporting detail: a real example, decision, artifact, observation, limitation, or counterexample. The writer should preserve the expert's level of certainty and terminology. If an answer is anecdotal, describe it as an example rather than silently generalizing it into a universal rule. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Prefer “because” over unsupported assertion. Instead of writing “teams should use phased migration,” turn the expert's explanation into a concrete sequence: what triggers the phase, what risk the phase controls, what signal indicates readiness to continue, and when the advice does not apply. That transformation produces information readers can use without fabricating a case study. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Editorial integration

First-party insight is strongest when attached to the exact decision it informs. A quote about deployment friction belongs next to the implementation choice it explains; a failed experiment belongs next to the conditions that caused it; a counterexample belongs where a simplistic recommendation would otherwise mislead. Keep the source note so editors can return to the transcript or knowledge document during fact-checking. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete scenario

An operations director explains why a warehouse automation project failed: the team optimized picking speed before validating item-location accuracy. The writer should preserve the sequence and the operational lesson, while separating it from any broader industry claim. A useful section might explain the dependency between location accuracy and throughput, then identify the conditions under which the lesson applies. It should not invent a second warehouse story or claim that the interview proves a universal causal law. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes

Do not polish the expert into a fictional omniscient narrator. Do not add credentials, customer names, performance numbers, or first-person language that were never supplied. Do not extract “unique insight” merely by changing the expert's vocabulary. If the answer is thin, ask a follow-up question or mark the gap; the absence of evidence is itself useful editorial information. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
