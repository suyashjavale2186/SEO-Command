# Competitor Link Gap

Use this reference to turn competitor backlink data into **qualified prospects**, not a copy-and-paste acquisition list. A linking domain is interesting because of the context in which it linked to a comparable resource, not merely because the competitor has the link. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Prospecting method
Choose page-level competitors first. Export or inspect referring pages, then classify each prospect by topical fit, audience overlap, editorial purpose, link placement, and whether the target page actually solved the reader's problem. Prioritize prospects where the competitor's link appears because of a resource, research finding, tool, expert contribution, or partnership that your business could legitimately recreate. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete example
Three competitors in a payroll category are cited by a professional-accounting association. Inspection shows each citation points to a genuinely useful payroll-compliance resource. The prospect is valuable because the association curates material for its audience, not because “competitor has link” is itself a qualification. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Outreach decision
For each prospect, choose the actual reason to contact: replace an outdated resource, contribute a missing expert perspective, provide original data, or suggest a genuinely useful tool. If none applies, do not pitch. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
Do not assume every competitor link is reproducible, automate generic outreach to an entire export, or interpret third-party domain metrics as proof of editorial value. Link acquisition tactics should be evaluated for spam-policy risk and business fit. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Evidence boundary
Observed competitor links are evidence of opportunity, not guarantees of acquisition or ranking benefit. Undocumented link-system claims are **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
## Practical handoff for competitor link gap
The analyst should leave a compact handoff that identifies the exact artifact, the evidence owner, the user or business outcome at stake, and the next validation action. For **competitor link gap**, record the observable state before interpretation so that a second practitioner can reproduce the decision without relying on the author's memory. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Use a topic-specific stop condition: pause when the evidence source is stale, the artifact is not comparable to the benchmark, or the proposed action depends on an undocumented Google mechanism. When an action is approved, preserve the rationale and the uncertainty so a later measurement cycle can test whether the decision was useful. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
