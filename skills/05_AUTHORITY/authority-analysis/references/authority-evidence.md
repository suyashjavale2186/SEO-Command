## External authority evidence

Authority analysis here focuses on **external evidence around the site and its link ecosystem**, not on whether an author has relevant credentials. Inspect who references the site, why they link, whether those sources are contextually relevant, and whether the relationship appears editorial, commercial, navigational, or manipulative. Third-party authority metrics can be used as comparative observations, but they are not official Google scores. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

### Evidence classes

- **Editorial citations:** references from relevant publications or organizations where the link supports a substantive statement. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
- **First-party relationships:** partner, association, event, or customer references that can be verified as legitimate relationships. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
- **Brand/entity references:** mentions that corroborate organization identity, while avoiding claims about guaranteed Knowledge Graph effects. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]
- **Third-party metrics:** DR/DA-like scores used only as vendor-specific comparative indicators, not Google ranking measurements. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
- **Spam signals:** patterns that resemble paid/manipulative link acquisition and therefore require policy review. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

### Red flags

Counting every referring domain equally, treating a proprietary metric as a Google score, using link quantity as proof of topical authority, or citing a single high-authority domain as proof of causation should trigger reanalysis. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Recommendations must explain the observed evidence and identify whether the conclusion is Official, Empirical, Observational, or Hypothesis. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Operator notes
For every substantive claim, trace it to a source and classify the support: Tier 1 official documentation, Tier 2 strong empirical evidence, Tier 3 industry observation, or Tier 4 hypothesis. If a source mixes observation and speculation, classify the relevant sentence at the weaker level rather than inheriting the source’s confidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Applied scenario
A vendor article says “our pages rank because of X.” The evidence engine may retain the vendor’s observation as Tier 3, but it must not restate an inferred private ranking mechanism as fact. If the mechanism cannot be independently supported, the recommendation should focus on the observable practice rather than the claimed internal signal. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Stop conditions
Upgrading evidence because a claim is repeated across many sites; treating a tool score as official Google data; hiding uncertainty in footnotes; or allowing a hypothesis to become a fact through copied templates. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
