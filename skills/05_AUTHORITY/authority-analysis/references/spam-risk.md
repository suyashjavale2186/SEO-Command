# Spam Risk in Authority Analysis

Use this reference to decide whether a link-acquisition pattern deserves investigation for **spam-policy or manipulation risk**. The analyst should describe the observable pattern and policy concern without pretending to know how an undisclosed ranking system will react. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## What to inspect
For each suspicious source, record the referring URL, target URL, anchor, placement, editorial context, domain purpose, and apparent reason the link exists. Then ask whether the link reflects a genuine editorial relationship or appears to have been created primarily to manipulate ranking signals. Google's spam policies prohibit links created primarily to manipulate rankings. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

Do not infer risk from one dimension alone. A sudden cluster may have a legitimate explanation such as a launch or news event; a stable pattern of unrelated coupon pages, paid placements without appropriate handling, or automated directory submissions deserves a different review. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Concrete scenario
A software company receives many links from unrelated coupon and low-purpose sites within a short campaign. The analyst maps the referring pages and discovers nearly identical anchors and repeated commercial placement language. The finding is recorded as a manipulation-risk pattern, not as proof of a hidden penalty. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Failure modes
Calling every low-authority domain “spam”; assuming an algorithmic penalty has occurred without evidence; using third-party toxicity scores as Google measurements; or recommending more links to dilute a pattern that should instead be stopped and reviewed. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Policy applicability can be documented; future ranking consequences usually cannot. Treat unsupported causal claims about Google's enforcement systems as **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Pattern review
Compare suspicious links by referring-page purpose, anchor context, destination, editorial rationale, and acquisition event. The analysis should distinguish a legitimate publicity surge from a repeated pattern of low-purpose pages created primarily for ranking manipulation. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

Do not label a link “toxic” because a vendor score says so. Vendor metrics can support triage, but policy classification should rely on observable behavior and published guidance. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
