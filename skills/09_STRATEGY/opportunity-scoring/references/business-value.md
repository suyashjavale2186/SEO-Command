# Business Value in Opportunity Scoring

This reference defines **one factor inside the Opportunity Score**: how commercially meaningful the topic would be if the organization captured the relevant demand. It should not duplicate the conversion model's event definitions. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Scoring logic
Evaluate whether the opportunity reaches the right buyer, supports a real product or service, aligns with margin or strategic importance, and can influence a meaningful commercial decision. A broad informational topic can score lower than a narrower query with strong product fit even when the broad topic has more visible demand. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Use evidence such as sales priorities, product margins, customer segments, CRM outcomes, and existing organic conversions. Do not assign a high business-value score because an executive likes the topic or because a competitor ranks for it. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Concrete scenario
A cybersecurity company compares “what is ransomware” with “ransomware incident response software.” The first query may have much broader reach, but the second is directly connected to a product and qualified buyer. The opportunity model records those facts separately; business value is only one factor and does not override feasibility or information advantage. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
Using revenue assumptions without source data, treating brand visibility as revenue, ignoring negative unit economics, or letting one executive priority determine the score without evidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Business value is an internal decision factor, not a Google ranking signal. The parent skill must preserve the distinction and use the required recommendation format. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Evidence worksheet
Use business value evidence from product priorities, sales conversations, margin logic, qualified pipeline, existing organic conversions, or documented customer demand. Separate observed business importance from a forecast about future revenue. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

The opportunity score becomes misleading when business value is treated as a proxy for popularity. A small, high-fit topic can deserve investment even when it lacks broad demand; conversely, a large informational topic may be strategically weak when the business has no useful next action for that audience. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
