# Roadmap Framework


**Purpose.** This reference gives the `seo roadmap` skill a concrete operating method for **roadmap framework**. Use it as an internal diagnostic and decision framework; it does not represent a hidden Google ranking formula. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


## Core principles


- Organize workstreams by objective, owner, dependency, expected outcome, evidence, and validation method. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


- Record evidence before scoring or recommending changes. The strongest evidence is primary documentation, direct first-party data, reproducible measurement, or clearly attributed expert evidence; weaker evidence should remain labeled as such. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]


- Keep observed facts, interpretations, forecasts, and hypotheses in separate fields. This prevents a plausible explanation from becoming an unsupported fact merely through repetition. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


- Never invent first-person experience, customer results, credentials, testimonials, measurements, or screenshots. When first-hand evidence is unavailable, write from sourced information and say what is missing. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Field procedure
Order work by dependencies, risk, capacity, and learning value. Technical blockers precede downstream content work when they can invalidate it; measurement instrumentation precedes experiments that depend on the data; evidence collection can precede expensive production. Keep milestones tied to a decision or deliverable rather than an arbitrary calendar ritual. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete case
A site wants a content expansion but its canonical/indexation issues are unresolved and Search Console segmentation is incomplete. The roadmap places instrumentation and technical cleanup first, then the content expansion, so later results are interpretable. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
Scheduling every recommendation in parallel; ignoring dependencies; committing to dates before inputs are ready; or treating a roadmap as a promise of rankings rather than an execution plan. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
