# Dependencies

**Purpose.** This reference is the operating memory for `seo-roadmap` when the exact job is to **sequence SEO work around prerequisites and blocking relationships**. Use it to decide what evidence to gather, how to act on the named artifact, and what would invalidate the conclusion. It is not a claim about an undisclosed ranking formula and it does not guarantee an outcome. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Use conditions

The unit of work is **dependencies**, not “SEO” in general. Before opening a checklist, name the URL, dataset, query group, experiment, stakeholder decision, or outreach asset under review. Record the state that exists now and the state the business or user actually needs. The distinction matters: a technically clean implementation can still be the wrong action if it serves the wrong audience, market, or commercial objective. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Investigation route

- map data, engineering, content, legal, and stakeholder dependencies [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
- identify actions that unlock several downstream items [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
- add fallback paths when a dependency owner cannot deliver [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Use the action list as a sequence of observations rather than a quota. Preserve source URLs, screenshots, exports, request/response evidence, query groups, or stakeholder records that another practitioner could inspect. When first-hand evidence is unavailable, do not manufacture first-person experience, customer results, credentials, measurements, or testimonials. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Specific example

A category-page redesign depends on URL mapping, analytics tagging, and template QA; publishing the content before those are ready would make later measurement and migration control harder. The example is hypothetical unless the operator attaches an identifiable real source. Treat it as a pattern for reasoning, not evidence that the tactic caused a particular result. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Invalid patterns

['sequencing by calendar alone', 'hiding cross-team blockers', 'treating optional polish as a prerequisite'] [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

A second class of error is methodological: confusing an observed sequence with a causal result, using a vendor benchmark as though it were universal, or converting an industry observation into a documented Google requirement. When that happens, downgrade the statement to the weaker evidence tier or remove it. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]

## Audit note

Before handing the result to the parent skill, attach the source or measurement that supports each material conclusion and state the next validation step. Keep the recommendation separate from the evidence that motivates it. If the recommended action involves search policy, structured data, redirects, links, or generative-search visibility, verify the current official guidance before implementation. Do not imply that a structured-data field, content pattern, or outreach tactic guarantees rankings, citations, traffic, or revenue. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Strategic dependency graph

This roadmap reference handles **cross-project prerequisites**, not the publication checklist for one asset. Map relationships such as “technical template repair unlocks category expansion,” “Search Console baseline must exist before an experiment,” or “new product availability is required before targeting a market.” Strategic dependencies are useful because they determine sequencing across workstreams. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Represent each dependency with a source task, blocked task, owner, decision date, and fallback path. Look for leverage: one engineering fix that unlocks many pages can justify earlier placement than a collection of independent editorial improvements. Conversely, a dependency owned by another team may require a parallel contingency rather than a passive wait. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Worked case

A marketplace roadmap includes category expansion, international launch, and content refresh. The international launch depends on localized inventory and language QA; category expansion depends on stable faceted navigation. The roadmap sequences both technical prerequisites early while allowing independent content work to proceed in parallel. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes

Do not confuse a preferred sequence with a hard dependency. Do not create circular dependencies to defend a favorite project. Do not hide external-team ownership. Do not declare an item blocked when a lower-risk fallback can preserve progress. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
