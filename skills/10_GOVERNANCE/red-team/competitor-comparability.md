# Competitor Comparability Red Team

This reference attacks a common strategic error: treating any site visible in a SERP as an interchangeable competitor. The red-team job is to test whether the chosen benchmark is actually comparable before another skill copies its content, authority pattern, format, or performance. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Challenge dimensions
Test **intent**, **audience**, **market**, **business model**, **page role**, **brand/entity prominence**, **resource constraints**, and **evidence availability**. A government guide may answer the same query while having a fundamentally different trust position and business purpose from a commercial vendor. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

Ask the planner to prove each comparator with a page-level reason. “It ranks above us” is an observation, not a sufficient explanation. Where possible, retain one alternative comparator and explain what would change the interpretation. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Red-team scenario
For a payroll query, the shortlist contains a payroll vendor, an independent software review, and a government employment guide. The team proposed copying all three. The red team rejects the plan: the vendor is a product comparator, the review is a commercial-content comparator, and the government guide is a factual authority. Each can inform a different part of the strategy, but none should be treated as one template. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Failure modes
Selecting only famous domains, mistaking position for causation, ignoring local or audience differences, copying features that cannot be supported internally, and turning one competitor's structure into a sitewide rule. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Any claim that a competitor outranks because of a private Google mechanism must be labeled **Tier 4 — Hypothesis** unless officially documented. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
