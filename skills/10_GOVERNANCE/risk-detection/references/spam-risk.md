# Spam Risk Taxonomy

This reference is a **governance gate**, not a backlink audit. Its purpose is to stop a proposed SEO tactic when the tactic's mechanics create an unacceptable policy, integrity, or reputational risk. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Risk classes
Classify the tactic as **editorial manipulation**, **automated/scaled generation**, **deceptive representation**, **link manipulation**, **cloaking or access manipulation**, **user-harmful behavior**, or **uncertain pending evidence**. Use the applicable published policy language where possible and do not replace a policy question with a vendor “risk score.” [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Governance procedure
Describe what the tactic actually does, who benefits, how it is implemented, and what evidence proves that description. Then test it against Search Essentials and relevant spam-policy guidance. If the tactic's defense is simply “everyone does this,” fail the gate. If the policy relevance is ambiguous, escalate rather than asserting certainty. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Concrete scenario
A team proposes mass-producing near-identical city pages with swapped place names and thin local detail. The red-team review flags scaled-content risk because the proposed mechanism multiplies pages without a demonstrated need for each page. The appropriate decision is to stop and establish genuine local value before publication. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Failure modes
Using a traffic win as evidence of policy compliance, assuming an absence of manual action means the tactic is safe, hiding the production method from reviewers, or labeling normal editorial marketing as spam without examining intent and implementation. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Evidence boundary
Spam-policy classification is grounded in published policy. Any claim about an undocumented enforcement mechanism or future ranking outcome is **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Review gate
Before approving a tactic, record the tactic's actual mechanism, intended purpose, implementation method, and relevant policy section. If the tactic depends on deception, scaled repetition without clear user value, or links created primarily to manipulate ranking signals, route it to a human reviewer rather than normalizing the behavior because it is common in the industry. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

Separate **policy risk** from **ranking risk**. A published policy can support a compliance warning; it cannot be used to assert that a specific hidden ranking response will occur. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
