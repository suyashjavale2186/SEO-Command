# Seo Risk Taxonomy


**Purpose.** This reference gives the `risk detection` skill a concrete operating method for **SEO risk taxonomy**. Use it as an internal diagnostic and decision framework; it does not represent a hidden Google ranking formula. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


## Core principles


- Classify risks as policy/spam, technical, content quality, authority, measurement, business, reputational, and operational. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


- Record evidence before scoring or recommending changes. The strongest evidence is primary documentation, direct first-party data, reproducible measurement, or clearly attributed expert evidence; weaker evidence should remain labeled as such. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]


- Keep observed facts, interpretations, forecasts, and hypotheses in separate fields. This prevents a plausible explanation from becoming an unsupported fact merely through repetition. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


- Never invent first-person experience, customer results, credentials, testimonials, measurements, or screenshots. When first-hand evidence is unavailable, write from sourced information and say what is missing. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Control points
Classify risk by mechanism: search-spam risk, scaled low-value content risk, implementation risk, attribution risk, user-trust risk, or policy/compliance risk. State the trigger, the evidence, the potential consequence, the containment step, and the owner who must approve or stop the tactic. Risk detection should be prospective where possible. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Production example
A campaign proposes hundreds of near-identical partner pages with commercial links. The screen records duplication, scale, editorial-purpose concerns, and the proposed stop condition, then requires a redesign around genuine audience value before launch. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Failure signatures
Using a risk score as a substitute for policy review; continuing a prohibited tactic after cosmetic changes; assuming an agency’s process is compliant because it is common; or calling a risky pattern a guaranteed penalty. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Topic-specific operating notes
Treat **seo risk taxonomy** as the concrete object of analysis within **risk-detection**. Work from the artifact that the file names—rather than from a generic SEO checklist—and preserve the field-level evidence that another practitioner would need to reproduce the judgment. For this topic, inspect the relevant SEO risk identification, implementation risk, spam exposure, scaled content, and reversibility signals first, then test the business or user consequence of the observed issue. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

When the evidence is incomplete, name the missing field or source explicitly and state what observation would change the conclusion. Do not substitute a vendor benchmark, an internal score, or a plausible Google explanation for missing evidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

### Topic-specific failure modes
Watch for the characteristic mistake of **seo risk taxonomy**: applying the concept to the wrong artifact, treating a proxy metric as the outcome, copying a competitor pattern without proving comparability, or turning a hypothesis into an assertion. Also stop when the proposed action would require fabricated evidence, violate search-policy guidance, or imply a guaranteed ranking or revenue result. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
