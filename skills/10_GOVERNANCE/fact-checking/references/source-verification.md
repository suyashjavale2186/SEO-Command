# Source Verification

**Scope.** `fact-checking/source-verification` answers whether a specific factual statement is adequately supported for publication. It works claim-first: identify the proposition, identify the jurisdiction and date, inspect the strongest available source, and decide whether the wording should be accepted, narrowed, attributed, or removed. It is not a general policy for ranking sources. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Claim decomposition

Break compound sentences into atomic claims. “The regulator requires X, the rule changed last year, and this applies to all businesses” contains separate propositions that may have different sources. Verify the exact wording, scope, exceptions, effective date, and applicability. When the source says “may,” do not rewrite it as “must.” When the source applies only to one jurisdiction, do not publish it as universal. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Verification route

Start with a primary source when the proposition concerns law, policy, product behavior, first-party data, or an official Search policy. Use independent empirical research when the claim is about an observed effect. Industry articles can help locate evidence, but they should not silently become the evidence of record for a high-consequence claim. Save the source and the exact passage that supports the statement. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

When sources disagree, do not average them into a synthetic “truth.” Record the conflict, compare dates and scope, and escalate the wording. A safer sentence may attribute the claim to a named authority or describe the disagreement explicitly. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Concrete case

A financial guide says, “Businesses must file form X every quarter.” The checker verifies the current official guidance and discovers that the requirement depends on turnover and registration status. The published claim is narrowed to the relevant conditions, with the official source attached. A vendor article that made the blanket statement is retained only as a lead, not as the final authority. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Failure modes

Never treat a search-result snippet as complete evidence. Never verify a citation without opening the cited source. Never let a source's prestige substitute for scope checking. Never invent a citation, date, quote, measurement, or expert statement. If verification cannot be completed, label the claim unresolved and prevent the writing workflow from presenting it as settled fact. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]
