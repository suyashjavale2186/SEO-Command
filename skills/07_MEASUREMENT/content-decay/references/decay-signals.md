# Decay Signals

This reference identifies **observable signals that a page may be losing usefulness or search visibility**. The measurement skill owns detection; content-update decisions should use that evidence rather than assuming that every decline needs a rewrite. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Signal groups
**Search demand capture:** impressions, clicks, CTR, and query mix can reveal that a page is capturing less of the opportunity it previously reached. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

**Ranking context:** a persistent decline across important queries can indicate stronger competition, changed intent, weaker relevance, stale facts, or technical delivery problems. Rank movement alone does not identify the cause. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

**Content freshness:** changed pricing, product features, regulations, dates, screenshots, references, or business processes can make previously accurate content misleading. Freshness matters when the underlying topic changes; freshness itself is not an automatic ranking lever. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

**Business outcome:** a page can retain traffic while losing qualified leads or sales if the audience or offer changed. Measurement should therefore preserve the page-to-conversion relationship. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Concrete scenario
A tax-software guide's impressions fall while competitors add current filing rules and examples. The analyst observes the change in query mix and checks whether the page's core claims are still current. The diagnosis points to stale guidance, not “Google punished old content.” [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Failure modes
Calling seasonality decay, using one metric in isolation, treating a competitor change as proof of causation, and deleting a page before checking historical conversion value. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]

## Evidence boundary
Decay signals establish that something changed; they do not identify a hidden ranking cause. Such causal explanations require separate evidence and, where undocumented, are **Tier 4 — Hypothesis**. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Baseline comparison
A decay alert should preserve a comparable historical baseline and explain why the comparison window is valid for the topic. Seasonal travel, tax, education, and retail queries can move for reasons unrelated to page quality, so the measurement record should note demand context before escalating to content changes. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

Classify the signal as demand change, SERP change, content staleness, technical degradation, or uncertain. Only the last category should move directly to further diagnosis rather than a predetermined edit. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]
