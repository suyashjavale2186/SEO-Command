# Landing-Page Analysis

This reference examines **what happens after an organic visitor lands on a page**. It belongs to analytics, not Search Console, so the unit of analysis is user behavior and business progression rather than search visibility. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Analysis sequence
Start with the landing page and acquisition context. Compare engaged behavior, relevant events, navigation to high-intent pages, form starts, qualified actions, and revenue or pipeline where available. Segment by query intent or page group when the data connection is reliable. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

Interpret behavior in context. A short session on a page designed to answer one factual question can be successful; a short session on a complex product-selection page may indicate a problem. Do not use one engagement metric as a universal quality score. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Concrete scenario
A SaaS guide attracts organic traffic but users rarely reach the comparison page. The analyst inspects the guide's calls to action, page structure, and navigation path and finds that the comparison link is buried after a long unrelated section. The proposed change is evaluated as a journey improvement, not as an attempt to manipulate a search metric. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Failure modes
Counting every short session as failure, treating bounce-style metrics as direct ranking factors, mixing audiences, and claiming conversion causation without an attribution model. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Evidence boundary
Analytics measures behavior within the configured system; it does not explain a hidden Google mechanism. Causal claims require appropriate controls and are labeled according to the evidence available. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Landing-page worksheet
For each organic landing page, preserve the acquisition context, page purpose, important next action, relevant event path, and downstream business outcome. Then compare audience segments only when the analytics configuration can support that separation. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

Interpret the page according to its job. A dictionary-style page may satisfy the visitor immediately, while a product comparison should usually support a later decision. The analyst should therefore judge behavior against page intent rather than applying one universal engagement benchmark. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]
