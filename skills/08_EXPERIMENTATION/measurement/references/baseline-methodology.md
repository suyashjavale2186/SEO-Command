# Baseline Methodology


**Purpose.** This reference gives the `measurement` skill a concrete operating method for **baseline methodology**. Use it as an internal diagnostic and decision framework; it does not represent a hidden Google ranking formula. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


## Core principles


- Build baselines from comparable periods, page sets, query groups, and known seasonality before interpreting change. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- Record evidence before scoring or recommending changes. The strongest evidence is primary documentation, direct first-party data, reproducible measurement, or clearly attributed expert evidence; weaker evidence should remain labeled as such. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]


- Keep observed facts, interpretations, forecasts, and hypotheses in separate fields. This prevents a plausible explanation from becoming an unsupported fact merely through repetition. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


- Never invent first-person experience, customer results, credentials, testimonials, measurements, or screenshots. When first-hand evidence is unavailable, write from sourced information and say what is missing. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Decision procedure
Choose a baseline that resembles the post-change state: same URL group, comparable query set, similar seasonal context, stable measurement definitions, and a known pre-change period. Record exclusions for outages, migrations, or unusual demand shocks. A baseline is a comparison frame, not a promise that the future would otherwise have remained constant. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Case study
An internal-link test begins after a site redesign, making the previous month a poor baseline. The analyst instead uses a cleaner historical period and documents the redesign as a confounder, then interprets the result conservatively. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Common failure modes
Using the immediately preceding period automatically; mixing pages with different intent; overlooking tracking migrations; or choosing a baseline because it makes the expected improvement look larger. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]
