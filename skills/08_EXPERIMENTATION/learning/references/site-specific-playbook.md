# Site-Specific Playbook


**Purpose.** This reference gives the `learning` skill a concrete operating method for **site-specific playbook**. Use it as an internal diagnostic and decision framework; it does not represent a hidden Google ranking formula. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


## Core principles


- Store validated patterns by template, query class, audience, and change type with the evidence behind each lesson. [Evidence Tier: Tier 3 — Industry observation]


- Record evidence before scoring or recommending changes. The strongest evidence is primary documentation, direct first-party data, reproducible measurement, or clearly attributed expert evidence; weaker evidence should remain labeled as such. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]


- Keep observed facts, interpretations, forecasts, and hypotheses in separate fields. This prevents a plausible explanation from becoming an unsupported fact merely through repetition. [Evidence Tier: Tier 4 — Hypothesis]


- Never invent first-person experience, customer results, credentials, testimonials, measurements, or screenshots. When first-hand evidence is unavailable, write from sourced information and say what is missing. [Evidence Tier: Tier 1 — Officially documented]

## Diagnostic sequence
Record what the intervention taught the team, not just whether the KPI moved. Preserve the original hypothesis, change, evidence, actual result, competing explanations, confidence, and transferability. A useful learning states where the result may apply and where it should not be generalized. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## Worked situation
A title experiment improves CTR for comparison pages but not for support documentation. The site-specific playbook records the result as format-dependent rather than turning it into a global rule for every page type. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]

## What breaks the tactic
Saving only the winning variant; deleting failed experiments; treating one site’s result as universal Google behavior; or transferring a lesson to another query class without comparable evidence. [Evidence Tier: Tier 2 — Strong empirical evidence]
